Selecting, rectangles and many cursors

How to pick out text, a block of columns, or many places at once.

Most commands in an editor act on something: a word to delete, a line to copy, a block to indent. A selection is the text the next command acts on. It shows in the :region face, usually as reversed colours. This chapter teaches how to make a selection by hand, by the shape of the text and by the shape of the code. It then goes on to blocks of columns, called rectangles, and to working in many places at once, with many cursors or with a pattern that holds every match.

Chapter 4 showed what d, y and p do with a selection. Chapter 2 showed how to move without selecting. Here the keys only make the selection, and a few commands that work on it follow.

5.1 Selections and the mark

Tuatara has two ways to select, and both end up as the same thing.

The Emacs way uses the mark. The mark is a second place in the buffer, kept beside the cursor. The region is the text between the mark and the cursor. C-SPC sets the mark where the cursor is and shows the region, and every move after that stretches it. C-g lets go. C-x C-x swaps the cursor and the mark, which is how you reach the other end of a region to change it. Chapter 2 describes the mark and the mark ring, the list of places the mark has been.

The NORMAL state way has keys for it. v starts a selection at the cursor and any move stretches it. Pressing v again while it is live starts it afresh from where the cursor is now. V selects whole lines instead. % selects the whole buffer. You can use whichever you like in either state, and a selection made by one is a region for the other.

Figure 5.1 shows a selection made with v and one made with V.

With a selection live, most letter keys act on it: d cuts it, c cuts it and starts INSERT state, y copies it. A line selection is widened to whole lines before it is cut or copied, so a deleted line leaves no empty line behind.

Try it, with a recipe

  1. Press C-x C-f, type ~/pancakes.txt and press RET. Press i and type Pancakes, RET, 2 eggs, 200 g flour, 1 cup milk, RET, Mix and rest., RET, Fry in butter. Press C-g.
  2. Press g g j to go to the second line. Press v, then w w. The first words of the line are selected. Press C-g to let go.
  3. Press V. The whole line is selected. Press J. The line below is selected too. Press d. Both lines are cut. Press u (non-modal: C-/) to undo.
  4. Press V on the second line, then K: the line above joins the selection. Press C-g. (K alone, with nothing selected, only moves up.)
  5. Press %. The whole buffer is selected. Press C-g.

5.5 Rectangles

A rectangle is a block of columns over several lines. It is the right tool for a column of numbers, a bullet at the start of each line, or text that lines up. Figure 5.2 shows one.

A rectangle covers the columns between the cursor and the mark on every line from one to the other. A line that ends inside the block gives only the part it has. The character under the cursor is part of the block, so three l (non-modal: C-f) presses from the first column cover four columns.

5.5.1 Rectangles in NORMAL state

m v (non-modal: C-x r SPC) starts a rectangle in NORMAL state, and any move sizes it. y copies the block, d cuts it and c cuts it and starts INSERT state with a cursor on every row, so that what you type goes on every line. P (non-modal: C-x r y) pastes the block back as a block, one row to a line. m v again, or C-g, ends the rectangle without doing anything.

Try it, with a price list

  2. Press g g g h, then l (non-modal: C-f) eight times. The cursor is on the 4. Press m v (non-modal: C-x r SPC), then j j j l l l. The four prices are the block. The echo area says any move sizes the block.
  3. Press y. The echo area says it copied a rectangle of four rows. Press g e and P (non-modal: M->, C-x r y). The prices are pasted again at the end of the last line, one row to a line, below it. Press u (non-modal: C-/) to undo.
  4. Press g g g h and l eight times, then m v j j j l l l and d. The prices are cut. Press g g g h and P. The column of prices has moved to the front of the lines. Press u twice to bring it all back.
  5. With the block made again, press c, type n/a and press C-g twice. Every price is now n/a. Press u.

5.6 Many cursors

t puts another cursor on the line below, at the same column. T puts one above. From then on, nearly every key you press works at every cursor. Move, select, type, delete: each key runs once at each place. This is the fast way to do the same small edit on many lines.

5.7 Splitting a selection on what matches

A pattern can make the cursors for you. Select some text and press f. Tuatara asks for a regular expression, a pattern as used in a search (chapter 6). It holds every match inside the selection at once, one place for each, and waits for you to say what to do with them.

ModalNon-modalCommandWhat it does with every match
inonesplit-insertTypes before every match.
anonesplit-appendTypes after every match.
cnonesplit-changeEmpties every match and types there.
dnonesplit-deleteDeletes every match.
a bracket or quotenonesplit-wrap-keyWraps every match in the pair. Press it again to take it off.
C-gnonesplit-quitLets go and ends the split.

Without a selection, f searches the line the cursor is on. If you put % in front of the pattern, as in %kereru, the whole buffer is searched whatever is selected. What you type at one match appears at all of them, and the writing is one change, so u (non-modal: C-/) takes it all back. Case is ignored when the pattern has no capital letter. As you type the pattern, the number of matches shows before the prompt.

If the selection is a rectangle, f searches its block. With a number first it searches each row's whole line.

Try it, with the recipe

  1. Open ~/pancakes.txt. Press g g j V to select the second line, the one with the quantities.
  2. Press f, type [0-9]+ and press RET. The three numbers are held. The echo area says how many places it holds and what you can do.
  3. Press (. Each number is wrapped in brackets. Press u.
  4. Press V, then f, type , and press RET. Press c, type ; and press C-g. The commas are now semicolons. Press u (non-modal: C-/).
  5. Press % and f, type pancake, press RET and then i. Type * and press C-g. The word Pancakes has a star in front of it. Press u.

f may miss a match of more than 4 KB that runs across several lines. For text where that matters, split-selection-thorough finds every match of up to a megabyte. It is slower, so it has no key of its own. A pattern with many matches is limited by *split-limit*, and *split-limit-ask* lets you hold them all even so.
